STOLEN REEL? GET IT REMOVED

What to do today when someone reposts your reel, how to keep proof that it's yours, what Instagram's new content protection will catch, and how the new Story edit works.

Step one: collect the proof (five minutes)
  • Copy the link of the stolen post: tap the three dots on it, then Copy link.
  • Copy the link of your original reel the same way.
  • Take screenshots of the stolen post that show the account name, the date and the view count.
  • Keep your original video file safe on your phone or drive. Its date proves you made it first.
  • Write one line on what was copied: the whole video, your voice, your script, or a scene.

Step two: report it the right way
  • Use Instagram's Copyright Report Form, not only the normal Report button. The normal button goes to general moderation; the form goes to the team that handles copyright.
  • Open it from the Instagram Help Center (help.instagram.com): search for Copyright Report Form.
  • Choose that you are the rights owner, paste the stolen post link and your original link, and describe what was copied.
  • Only report your own work. False reports can get your own account restricted.
  • If the first report is rejected, send it again with clearer proof: both links, screenshots and the original file date.

Step three: make your reels harder to steal
  • Put your handle on screen in every reel, in a place a crop can't remove easily.
  • Say your name in the reel. Your voice is your fingerprint, and that's exactly what Instagram's new tool listens for.
  • Post first on your own account, then share anywhere else.
  • Keep the original files with their dates. That's your proof if you ever need it.

Part two: the Story fix
  • Spotted a mistake in your Story after posting? Don't delete it. Deleting throws away all its views and replies.
  • Open the Story, tap the three dots and choose Edit.
  • You can change the text, the music, stickers and drawings for fifteen minutes after posting.
  • Your views, likes and replies stay as they are.
  • If you don't see Edit yet, update the Instagram app. New features reach accounts in stages.
