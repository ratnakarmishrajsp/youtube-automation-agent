PHONE PHOTO TO INSTAGRAM AD

Turn one plain phone photo of your product into a studio-style AI shot and a short AI video, then let Meta test it against your other creatives. All three prompts inside, plus the label check that stops embarrassing mistakes.

What you need
  • One clean phone photo of your product: daylight, a plain background, the label facing the camera. The clearer this photo is, the closer the AI keeps your real product.
  • Gemini (gemini.google.com or the app) for the AI product shot. Its image model is called Nano Banana. Free and paid plans get different daily image limits.
  • Google Flow (labs.google/flow) to turn that photo into a short video. Flow runs on credits: read the cost line before you press generate.
  • Meta Ads Manager to run the creatives as ads.

Prompt one: the AI product shot (Gemini, Nano Banana)
  • Upload your phone photo in Gemini and paste this:
  • Put this product in a model's hand, studio light.
Keep the label exactly the same.
A model holds this exact product at chest height, label facing the camera, soft smile, clean beige studio background, soft key light from the left, premium ad look, sharp focus on the product, vertical photo.
Do not change the product's shape, colours, cap or any letters on the label.
  • No model? Use the table version:
  • Place this exact product on a clean studio table with soft daylight and a plain light background, premium ad look, sharp focus on the product, vertical photo.

  • Make a few versions and keep the one that looks most real.

Prompt two: the label lock
  • The most important line is already inside prompt one: Keep the label exactly the same. Without it, AI rewrites the small letters on your label.
  • Honest warning from our own test: even with that line, Gemini still changed a few small words on a medicine label. So zoom in on every word. If anything is wrong, paste this in the same chat with your real label text:
  • The label text changed. Keep this exact image, same model, same pose, same light. Only fix the label so every word is exactly this:
[PASTE EVERY WORD OF YOUR LABEL HERE, LINE BY LINE]
  • Big text (brand name, product name) survives well. Tiny ingredient lists are where AI slips, so check them twice.

Prompt three: photo to video (Google Flow)
  • Open Flow, start a new project and pick Frames to Video. Put your checked AI shot in the start frame.
  • Settings that keep the cost low: Omni Flash, the shortest length you need, one output, vertical. Re-make only a take that failed, not all of them.
  • Paste this motion prompt:
  • Locked camera, almost no movement, only a very slow, subtle push-in.
The model holds the product perfectly still. The product does not move, tilt or rotate.
Soft studio light and background bokeh gently shimmer.
The label text stays perfectly sharp, still and unchanged. No new text, no logos.
  • Why a locked camera: when the camera moves fast, small label text melts. A slow, almost still shot keeps the letters readable.
  • Download the clip and watch it frame by frame at full size before you use it.

Let Meta pick the winner
  • In Ads Manager, put three ads in one ad set: the AI video, the AI photo and your original phone photo.
  • Keep the budget small and let Meta's delivery move spend to the ad that works. Give it a few days before you judge.
  • Look at two things: how many people stopped in the first seconds, and how many clicked or bought. Make next week's creatives in the style of the winner.
  • A new creative every week keeps your ads fresh, and you never book a shoot.

Three mistakes to avoid
  • Wrong label letters. A customer who sees a misspelled label stops trusting the product. Never publish a frame you haven't checked.
  • Strange hands or fingers. If a hand looks wrong, drop that shot. Don't hope nobody notices.
  • A product that looks better than the real one. Same size, same colour, same pack. If the ad promises more than the box, you get returns and bad reviews.
